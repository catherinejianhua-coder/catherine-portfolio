The Product Judgement

The useful product was not a contract chatbot. It was a repeatable decision system: capture legal positions in a playbook, let the model apply them clause by clause, and keep the reviewer in control of anything that carries judgement or context.

Problem

Legal teams spend too much time re-making familiar decisions on boilerplate contracts. The risk is not slow reading alone; it is letting repeated decisions remain trapped in individual reviewers' heads.

Decision

I made the playbook the real product. The model classifies clauses against standard positions, but every recommendation cites the rule it used and every override becomes a controlled playbook-change proposal.

Boundary

The tool never sends a counterparty response. A lawyer signs off the outcome, and auto-clear only becomes possible after shadow-mode evidence shows zero false greens on real historical contracts.

How The Workflow Works

The contract goes through a narrow, auditable path before any reviewer sees a draft response.

Step 1 Classify the contract

Template match, key facts and clause coverage are extracted so the reviewer knows what kind of review they are dealing with before reading line by line.

Step 2 Apply the legal playbook

Each clause is checked against written positions and fallbacks. The output is green, amber or red with a citation, not a free-form model opinion.

Step 3 Keep judgement human

Green contracts are candidates for later auto-clear; ambers produce editable amends; reds start with a human review brief. Nothing is sent by the system.

What The Evals Changed

The strongest part of this artefact is not the score. It is the place where the system failed and the product rule got tighter.

Initial smoke test

18-case golden set

The first run behaved coherently against the drafted playbook, including two deliberately borderline cases. That was useful as a regression test, but not enough to claim real-world readiness because the clauses were synthetic and the prompt, playbook and eval set were authored in the same working context.

Fresh-context failure

One stable false green

Three fresh-context reruns each scored 17/18 and put the same case through as green when it should have been amber. The clause text matched the fallback, but the fallback depended on commercial context the model could not see. The fix was a playbook rule: conditional fallbacks never auto-clear from text alone.

This is a portfolio artefact, not legal advice. Sample contracts are fictional; the value shown here is the product system: playbook governance, evaluation, reviewer workflow and controlled rollout.
